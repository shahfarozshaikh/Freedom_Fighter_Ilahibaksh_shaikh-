Biography

Late Ilahibaksh Mohammedsahab Shaikh was born in Solapur in 1920. From his youth, he was devoted to India's struggle for independence and to the welfare of society.

His life reflected courage, simplicity, honesty, service and strong moral values. He continued to care for people and the community, leaving behind a legacy remembered with pride by his family.

His Connection with the Freedom Movement

  • Worked with the Congress organisation from around the 1940s.
  • Participated in the Quit India Movement of 1942.
  • Was involved in underground freedom activities, including spreading and circulating awareness material.
  • Worked in and around the Solapur–Gulbarga region during the freedom struggle.
  • Also took part in activities connected with the wider Gandhian movement and its ideals of truth, non-violence and service.

The Freedom Struggle

His contribution was rooted in courage, sacrifice and service to the nation.

From the Historical Record

Available historical material records his association with Congress activities, participation during the 1942 movement, underground work and efforts to support the freedom movement despite police pressure.

He is remembered not only for the struggle for independence, but also for his commitment to social welfare and humanity.

Recognition & Support from the Government of Maharashtra

The family archive records several forms of recognition and support associated with his contribution as a freedom fighter.

✦

Freedom Fighter Pension

He received a freedom fighter pension from the Government of Maharashtra in recognition of his contribution.

🚌

Free Travel Facility

He was provided a free travel facility / bus pass as a freedom fighter.

🏅

Sanman Patra

He was honoured by the Government of Maharashtra with a Sanman Patra dated 7 December 1987.

◉

Spirit Business Licence

A spirit business licence was granted as a government benefit associated with his freedom-fighter recognition.

▣

Government Employment Benefit

A government employment opportunity was extended in the family in recognition of his service; the opportunity was given to his daughter, who became a teacher.
